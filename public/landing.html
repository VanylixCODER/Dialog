03 — SECURITY

Nobody in the middle of a call, and no fiction about the rest.

Call media never touches a server. Sessions can be told to expire and wipe the device. What isn't encrypted yet is written down plainly rather than glossed over — the code is public, so verify it instead of believing us.

Read the privacy notes
Calls are peer-to-peerAudio, video and screen share flow directly between participants over WebRTC. No media server sits in the middle, so no third party receives the stream.
Messages are not E2E encrypted — yetThey travel over TLS and are stored on the server you or we run. End-to-end encrypted DMs are on the roadmap below, and this line changes when they ship.
Self-destructing sessionsSet a device timer: when it runs out the account signs out and clears its local cache — storage, service worker and all.
No phone number, no ad graphA username is the whole identity. No contact upload, no tracking, no analytics SDKs.

08 — FAQ

Reasonable questions

Is it end-to-end encrypted?

Call media is: audio, video and screen share go straight between participants and never reach a server. Messages are not — they travel over TLS and are stored on the instance you or we run. End-to-end encrypted DMs are on the roadmap, and we would rather say so than imply otherwise.

What does the server know about me?

A username, a hashed password, your messages, and the routing metadata needed to deliver them. No phone number, no address book, no analytics. Self-host and that server is yours.

How big can a call get?

Calls are a peer-to-peer mesh — every participant sends to every other one. Small groups are comfortable; very large rooms are not what it is built for.

Is it free?

Yes, and open source under AGPL. No paid tier gating calls or screen share. If you would rather not use the hosted instance, run your own — same code, same clients.
